## Diagnosis

Segment: a first-time visitor who took the popup or footer signup for 10% and is browsing, possibly from the \$16 to \$39 best sellers. Awareness stage 2: they know the store; the doubt is "what do I buy here for my first order, and is \$250 a lot". Stage 2 task: extend where and when (the ear stack: one single now, more later, each piece a place on the ear). Sophistication 2 to 3 (labelled assumption: "curated fine jewelry" is common; the ear stack as a way to buy is the specific mechanism). One performance: start an ear stack with one single from \$400. Belief built on: a curated mix looks better than a matched set ("a real mix of classics, funky pieces, and vintage jewelry"). Belief routed around: fine jewelry has to be bought in pairs and big budgets. Belief left alone: price objections beyond showing real prices. Route: central (a considered \$400 to \$590 piece). Defensible claim: The Ear Edit singles from \$400.00, sold as singles, 10% off a first order of \$250 or more with the site's exclusions line, free ground shipping and returns on U.S. orders over \$250, Client Care to help choose. Structure: product demonstration (the ear map) with the offer and its condition stated up front. Levers, three: the discount (reason: first-time customer signing up), authority/liking (Laura, est. 2006, LA and NY boutiques), service as risk reduction (Client Care + free returns line). No reviews on site, so no social-proof lever.
