## Diagnosis

Segment: someone who typed an email into the footer form ("First time subscribers get 20% off their first order") or the popup, and got nothing back. Awareness stage 2: they know the brand and have not bought; the doubt is which bag, and whether a \$26 bag is worth it. Stage 2 task: sharpen the image (show what is in the Colombia bag and who roasted it). Sophistication 3 (labelled assumption: "freshly roasted, small batch, sustainably sourced" is what every roaster's welcome says, per the Really Good Emails set), so the mechanism goes in as named facts: roasted Monday - Friday in Chicago, grown at 1,500 to 2,070 meters, roasted by the 2025 US Coffee Roasting Champion. One performance: an everyday bag a champion roasted this week. Belief built on: fresh-roasted coffee tastes better. Belief routed around: specialty coffee is fussy. Belief left alone: price ("the price is a little high" appears in one review; not argued). Route: peripheral leaning central (\$26, a rebuy). Defensible claim: Colombia, \$26.00, 4.71 from 7 reviews, roasted by Diego, "crowned the 2025 US Coffee Roasting Champion", free shipping on all orders, 20% off a first order as the footer states it. Structure: offer ad with a proof stack (offer first because the reader asked for it, then the bag, then proof). Levers, three: the discount (reason: they signed up), authority (Diego's title), social proof (three verified reviews + the 4.71 counter). Left out: the subscription ladder, the Manos de Mujer cause story, wholesale.
