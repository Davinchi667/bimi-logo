## Diagnosis

Segment: a visitor who has tasted Pure Inventions water at a spa or resort, found the site, and would join a list if one existed (today they can only join at checkout). Awareness stage 2: they know the taste, not the brand or which bottle; the doubt is "is it worth \$24.99 for drops". Task (c), extend where and when: the spa water, at home, every day. Sophistication stage 3 (labelled assumption: water enhancers sit in every supermarket), so the reviewers' spa story and the cost per glass carry it, not a new claim. One performance: the water you liked at the spa, in your own bottle. Belief built on: it tasted good there. Belief routed around: drops are a supermarket squirt. Belief left alone: any health effect beyond the site's hydration line. Route: peripheral leaning central (a \$24.99 rebuy). Defensible claim: served in thousands of renowned spas and resorts; developed by two Certified Clinical Nutritionists since 2003; up to 30 beverages a bottle; free shipping over \$50. Structure: proof-led welcome with a recognition opening. Levers, three: social proof (the spa reviewers), authority (the nutritionist founders), value (cost per glass). Left out: Subscribe & Save (no % shown on the homepage), bundles, the benefits menu beyond P0.
