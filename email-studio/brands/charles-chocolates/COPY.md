## Diagnosis

Segment: a newsletter subscriber who joined for the free bar and has not bought. Awareness stage 2: knows the brand (Bay Area, gifts), deciding what to buy and whether the bar is real. Task: sharpen the image of the signature box and remove the doubt about the bar (one wording, the \$50 line made concrete). Sophistication 2 (labelled assumption: premium chocolate gifts are a crowded but claim-led field), so the real awards and press carry it. One performance: the box that started it all, as a gift. Belief built on: Charles Chocolates is a Bay Area name. Belief routed around: a free-bar promise that does not arrive (a review says "WE NEVER RECEIVE THE PROMISED CHOCOLATE BAR"). Left alone: price comparisons. Route: peripheral (\$31 to \$58, gift). Defensible claim: the Classic Collection, small-batch, made fresh in San Francisco, \$31 / \$58; "Best Chocolatier in the Bay Area" (San Francisco Magazine); a free bar with a first \$50+ purchase, non-cumulative. Structure: offer-led welcome with a product feature. Levers, three: the free bar (reason: they joined), authority (awards and press), social proof (gift reviews). Left out: Praline Crunch mix-and-match, Chocolate Club, corporate gifts, Dubai bars (strong press, but 1-star reviews in the latest 20; not the hero).
